# Handled. — Voice & Tone Guide

**What this is:** The website has already established a clear, distinctive voice.
This guide codifies it so *everyone* — writing landing copy, in-app text, error
messages, onboarding, billing emails, support replies — sounds like the same brand.
Voice is who we are and stays constant. Tone flexes with the situation.

Every example marked *(from the site)* is real copy from legalhandled.com. Use them
as the reference recording of how Handled. sounds.

This guide is the operational arm of [`/BRAND.md`](../BRAND.md) — especially
Principle 3 (*be who you say you are*) and Principle 4 (*do the right thing*).
For this voice applied to real in-app surfaces (empty states, errors, billing,
cancellation), see [`docs/in-app-microcopy.md`](in-app-microcopy.md).

## The voice in one line

> **Calm, plain-spoken confidence — a competent colleague who's got it handled,
> never a vendor making a pitch.**

We write for a **litigator**: time-starved, deadline-anxious, allergic to vendor
hype, and fluent in the language of matters, appearances, and discovery. Every word
should either lower their anxiety or respect their time. Ideally both.

---

## Six voice pillars

### 1. Calm, and it lowers anxiety
Our reader lives with the fear that something will slip. The voice is the antidote:
steady, quiet, resolved. The brand name is a full sentence ending in a period for a
reason — *done.*
- **We say:** "nothing slips and your evenings belong to you again." · "Rest easy.
  _It's handled._" *(from the site)*
- **Not:** breathless urgency, FOMO, "Don't miss out!", exclamation points as energy.

### 2. Confident, never boastful
Declarative and understated. We state the outcome plainly and let it stand. No
superlatives, no chest-beating.
- **We say:** "Every deadline, _accounted for._" · "One glance. Whole caseload."
  *(from the site)*
- **Not:** "best-in-class," "revolutionary," "the #1 tool for litigators,"
  "game-changing."

### 3. Radically honest — no hype, ever
This is our signature and our moat. We'd rather admit a limitation than dress it up.
Trust is the product in legal.
- **We say:** "We won't fake a track record. There isn't one yet." · "Straight
  answers." *(from the site)*
- **Not:** "trusted by thousands of attorneys" (we're pre-launch — it's a lie),
  invented testimonials, vague "bank-grade" claims we can't substantiate.

### 4. Concise — it respects their time
Short sentences. Deliberate fragments for rhythm. No filler, no throat-clearing. If
a word isn't working, cut it.
- **We say:** "Sticky notes, spreadsheets, and sheer memory." · "Start free. Upgrade
  when the caseload demands it." *(from the site)*
- **Not:** "In today's fast-paced legal environment, attorneys are increasingly
  finding that…"

### 5. Fluent in litigation — an insider, not a software company
Handled. was built by a practicing commercial litigator, and the copy proves it. We
use the real nouns of the work. This *is* the "wow" — the reader feels understood
before they're sold.
- **We say:** matters, deadlines, appearances, pleadings, discovery, motions,
  parties with counsel, negotiations, department. *(from the site)*
- **Not:** generic SaaS-speak — "projects," "tasks," "items," "users," "workflows,"
  "records" — when a precise litigation word exists.

### 6. On the attorney's side
Every choice visibly favors the customer: their data, their money, their exit. We
never trap, never obscure.
- **We say:** "Your data never leaves your desk." · "CSV export means it's never
  locked in." · "If Free does everything you need, stay on Free." *(from the site)*
- **Not:** dark patterns, guilt on cancellation, buried pricing, "contact sales."

## Signature devices

These are the mechanical fingerprints of the voice. Use them on purpose.

| Device | How it works | Example *(from the site)* |
|---|---|---|
| **The period on "Handled."** | The thesis as punctuation: finished, closed, calm. Never `Handled!` (hype) or `Handled` (unfinished). | "Rest easy. _It's handled._" |
| **Italic payoff word** | Emphasize the *feeling or outcome*, not the feature. | "Every deadline, _accounted for._" |
| **Deliberate fragments** | Two beats for rhythm and finality. | "One glance. Whole caseload." |
| **Antithesis / contrast** | Sharpen a claim by naming its opposite. | "Built for every kind of lawyer. Which means it was built for none." |
| **Concrete litigation nouns** | Specificity signals we know the work. | "Parties and counsel, key dates, court and department…" |
| **The middot meta line** | Terse, scannable reassurances separated by `·`. | "Free forever tier · macOS + Windows · Your data stays on your device" |

## Vocabulary

**Words that are ours:** handled, accounted for, nothing slips, rest easy, straight
answers, matter, caseload, appearance, deadline, litigator, local-first, your data
stays yours, start free, founding rate.

**Words we avoid:**
- **Hype:** revolutionary, game-changing, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class,
  powerful, robust, next-gen, supercharge, unleash, 10x, effortless.
- **Generic SaaS filler:** solution, platform, leverage, synergy, ecosystem,
  streamline (as filler), users (say *attorneys*), tasks/projects (say *matters*).
- **Unearned claims:** guaranteed, bulletproof, never fails, industry-leading,
  trusted by thousands. If it isn't literally true today, we don't say it.
- **Legalese in our own voice:** utilize, pursuant to, herein, leverage. Our audience
  is lawyers, but *we* speak plainly — that's the relief.

## Tone by context

Voice stays the same; **tone adjusts to what the attorney is feeling in the moment.**

| Context | The reader's state | Tone | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landing / marketing** | Skeptical, curious | Confident, calm, benefit-led | Slipping into hype or superlatives |
| **Onboarding / empty states** | New, uncertain | Encouraging, brief, points to the next action | Cuteness; walls of text |
| **Success / confirmations** | Relieved | Quietly satisfying — "It's handled." | Over-celebrating; confetti energy |
| **Errors** | Anxious, possibly angry | Honest, calm, no blame. Say what happened + what to do + that their data is safe | Jokes, jargon, vague "something went wrong" — *never* be flip when a deadline or matter is at stake |
| **Billing / cancellation** | Guarded | Gracious, plain, zero friction | Guilt, dark patterns, "are you sure?!" |
| **Security / privacy** | Evaluating trust | Precise and literal — claims must be exactly true | Vague reassurance; anything we can't back up |
| **Support replies** | Needs help, feels ignored | Human, straight, takes responsibility, closes the loop | Canned deflection; treating a complaint as a threat rather than a customer who cares |

## Worked rewrites

**Generic SaaS → Handled.**
> ❌ "Our revolutionary platform empowers legal professionals to seamlessly
> streamline their entire workflow!"
> ✅ "Case management that maps to how litigation actually moves."

**An error message**
> ❌ "Error 500: Something went wrong."
> ✅ "That didn't save. Your matter is intact — we just couldn't write the last
> change. Try again, or restore from your most recent backup."

**A cancellation screen**
> ❌ "Are you sure? You'll lose all your data forever!"
> ✅ "Cancel anytime. Export every matter to CSV first, and Free keeps everything
> you've already entered."

**An empty state**
> ❌ "No data to display."
> ✅ "No matters yet. Add your first one and the dashboard fills in."

**A feature line**
> ❌ "Robust, best-in-class deadline management with powerful automation."
> ✅ "Every deadline across every matter, ranked by urgency. What needs you today
> rises to the top."

## The 30-second checklist

Before anything ships, read it back and ask:

1. **Calm?** Would this lower a stressed litigator's blood pressure, or raise it?
2. **True?** Every claim literally accurate *today* — no borrowed track record?
3. **Tight?** Any word I can cut without losing meaning?
4. **Insider?** Real litigation nouns, not generic software-speak?
5. **On their side?** Does it obviously serve the attorney, not just us?
6. **Sounds like Handled.?** Read it aloud — is it a calm colleague, or a vendor
   pitching?

If any answer is no, it's not handled yet.
